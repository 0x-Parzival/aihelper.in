# AI Helper buyer archetypes

## Research basis

Business software purchases can involve several people with different responsibilities. Forrester describes champions, decision makers, ratifiers, users and other buying-group roles; Gartner reports that group-wide relevance helps consensus more than tailoring to one person's private motives. McKinsey's 2026 B2B Pulse also finds different interaction preferences across buyers, so a job title alone cannot determine how someone wants to buy. Salesforce's 2025 service survey identifies security as a frequent AI adoption concern. These findings support role-aware questions and explicit preference checks, not personality typing or claims about conversion probability.

## Working taxonomy

These are **AI Helper design hypotheses**, not categories established by the cited surveys. They cover the main responsibilities and evaluation jobs for a voice-agent purchase. One person may hold several roles; `other` and `unknown` prevent forced classification.

| Archetype | Relevant conversation focus |
| --- | --- |
| Owner/operator | Interruptions, missed enquiries, simple setup, ownership of follow-up |
| Revenue leader | Lead speed, qualification, follow-up and what makes a lead useful |
| Service leader | Routine caller needs, quality, escalation and review |
| Operations leader | Coverage, scheduling, consistency and handoffs across people or locations |
| Technical evaluator | Telephony, CRM, data flow, implementation and reliability requirements |
| Risk evaluator | Notice, consent, privacy, retention, access and escalation requirements |
| Financial buyer | Approved price, expected volume, present cost and measurable pilot criteria |
| Agency partner | Client ownership, separation, approvals and delivery responsibilities |
| Frontline champion | Day-to-day handoff quality, workload and adoption concerns |
| Buyer researcher | Decision criteria, evidence needs and other stakeholders |
| Other / unknown | Ask about their role and the business outcome before tailoring |

Jev classifies a **tentative current buying role** from the public transcript during AI Helper sales conversations. Its choice must meet the existing confidence validation. If it cannot, the last supported role or `unknown` remains; the core turn decision still works. The role informs one relevant question or explanation when appropriate. Direct questions, expressed preferences, opt-outs, approved prices, and verified capabilities always take precedence. The agent must never say the archetype label, infer it from voice or demographics, claim hidden insight about a person, or treat it as a likelihood to buy. Buyer interaction preference is taken from what the person asks for: human discussion, concise self-service facts, or a technical pilot. No survey percentage is used as an individual prediction.
